Getting your community live is step one. Growing it into something that earns predictable revenue — and keeps members renewing month after month — is the real game. The good news: Berrytap is built to make both easier. This guide covers how to find your first members, keep the ones you have, build a culture people don't want to leave, and price your membership in a way that rewards you for adding value over time.

## Getting your first 10 members

Your first 10 members are not a marketing problem — they're a relationships problem. Don't run ads, don't overthink your funnel. Do this instead:

**Start with personal outreach.** Write a short message to 20–30 people you know who would genuinely benefit from what you're building. Not a mass email — individual messages. Tell them what the community is, what they'll get, and invite them to be a founding member. Even a 20% conversion rate gets you into double digits.

**Share your community link everywhere you already have an audience.** Your community URL is **berrytap.com/c/your-community-name** — put it in your social bio, your email signature, your stories, your posts. Every piece of content you make is an opportunity to invite people in.

**Offer the first few spots free or at a founding price.** It's much easier to ask someone to try something free or discounted than to pay full price for something they haven't experienced yet. Let your first 5–10 members in at a founding rate, get them results, and use their experience as proof for everyone who comes after. You can always raise the price once you have social proof.

## Keeping members month after month

Churn happens when members feel like they're not getting value. The fix is almost always consistency — showing up regularly, adding content, and making the community feel alive.

**Post consistently in the feed.** Members notice when a community goes quiet. Even 3 posts per week — a question, a resource, a win — is enough to signal that the community is active and worth staying in. Make it a non-negotiable part of your week.

**Add new course content regularly.** A new lesson once a month gives members a concrete reason to stay. It doesn't have to be a full module — a short video, a new worksheet, a guest interview. The feeling that the community keeps growing is powerful.

**Run a monthly live session.** A live room Q&A or teaching session once a month is one of the highest-ROI things you can do to reduce cancellations. Members who show up to a live call almost never cancel that month. The act of attending creates a personal connection that passive content can't replicate.

<Tip>
  Run a **monthly live Q&A** — even just 30–45 minutes — and watch your cancellation rate drop. Members who attend feel seen and heard, and they're far more likely to renew. Announce it at the start of each month so members can plan to join.
</Tip>

**Use the leaderboard.** Berrytap tracks points, levels, and leaderboards automatically based on how members engage. Call out your top members publicly. Recognise them. Make being an active member feel good. Members who earn points and climb the leaderboard are deeply unlikely to cancel — they've invested themselves in the community.

<Note>
  Berrytap's built-in gamification — points, levels, and leaderboards — does a significant amount of the retention work for you, passively. Members who are engaged with the system have a reason to stay that goes beyond the content itself. You don't have to build or manage it; it runs automatically.
</Note>

## Using the feed to build culture

The feed isn't just a content channel — it's where your community's identity gets formed. How you use it shapes what your members think the community is for and how they behave in it.

**Pin a welcome post.** Make the first thing every new member sees a warm, clear post that explains what the community is about, what they should do first, and what to expect. This dramatically reduces drop-off in the first 48 hours.

**Run weekly questions.** A recurring post — "What's your win this week?" or "What are you working on?" — becomes a ritual. Members start to look forward to it. Rituals build culture.

**Spotlight members.** When a member does something notable — finishes a course, hits a milestone, shares something helpful — post about it. "Huge shoutout to [Name] for completing the full program this week 🎉" takes 30 seconds and means the world to that person. It also signals to everyone else what kind of community this is.

<Tip>
  Announce community milestones in the feed: "We just hit 50 members! 🎉" or "100 people have now completed the first module." These posts create a sense of momentum and belonging — members feel like they're part of something that's growing. It also attracts new members when you share these moments publicly.
</Tip>

## Pricing strategy: start lower, raise as you grow

The best time to raise your price is after you've added value — not before. Here's a practical framework:

1. **Launch at a founding price** — lower than where you eventually want to be. Reward early members for taking a chance on you.
2. **Lock in founding members at their price** — grandfather them in as you raise the price for new members. This rewards loyalty and makes founding membership feel genuinely special.
3. **Raise your price each time you add a meaningful new asset** — a new course, a new live program, a new tier of content. Price increases tied to value feel fair. Arbitrary increases don't.
4. **Announce the upcoming price increase publicly** — "Price goes up at the end of the month" creates urgency and drives a predictable spike in new members before each raise.

This approach means your revenue grows alongside your community, and your members feel like they're getting more over time rather than paying more for the same thing.

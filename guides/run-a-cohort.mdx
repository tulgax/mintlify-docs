A cohort is one of the most powerful ways to teach online. Everyone starts together, goes through the material together, and finishes together — with live sessions that keep the energy high and a community that makes the work feel less lonely. Completion rates are dramatically higher than self-paced courses, and the experience is memorable enough that graduates become your loudest advocates. This guide shows you exactly how to run one on Berrytap.

## What makes a cohort work

Three things: structured lessons that give participants a clear path, live rooms where real teaching and Q&A happens, and a community feed where the group builds momentum together. Berrytap has all three in one platform — which means your participants never have to switch tools, and you never have to wrangle multiple apps to run your cohort.

<Note>
  All course materials remain accessible to your members even after the cohort ends. Participants keep lifetime access to the lessons they worked through, so the value doesn't disappear when the program closes.
</Note>

## Run your cohort

<Steps>
  <Step title="Create your community and announce the start date">
    Head to [berrytap.com/register](https://berrytap.com/register) and create your community. Name it after your cohort (e.g. "Content Sprint — August Cohort") and include the start date and end date in the description. This builds urgency and tells potential members exactly what they're signing up for.

    Your community will be live at **berrytap.com/c/your-community-name**, so make the name something clean and shareable.
  </Step>

  <Step title="Build your course with a clear lesson structure">
    Create your course inside the community. Each **module** should represent a week (or a phase) of the cohort, and each **lesson** should be a single, completable piece of content — a video, a written guide, a worksheet, or a combination.

    You have two approaches to releasing lessons:

    - **All at once** — participants can move at their own pace, though your live sessions create a shared rhythm
    - **On a schedule** — new modules unlock each week, which keeps the cohort in sync and drives people to show up for live sessions when the material is fresh

    Either works. For tighter cohorts with a strong weekly structure, scheduled releases tend to create more group energy.
  </Step>

  <Step title="Schedule a weekly live room for teaching and Q&A">
    Set a recurring live session for the same day and time each week — for example, every Thursday at 8pm. Post this schedule as a pinned post in the feed before the cohort starts so participants know what to expect from day one.

    Use the live room for a mix of teaching the week's material, answering questions, and letting participants share their progress. These calls are the heartbeat of the cohort — don't skip them, and don't make them optional.
  </Step>

  <Step title="Set membership as one-time access">
    Since a cohort has a fixed end date, set your membership to **one-time access**. Participants pay once to join and get access for the duration of the cohort (plus lifetime access to the course materials after it ends).

    Price in MNT and choose a number that reflects the transformation you're delivering. QPay and card are available at checkout — your participants won't hit any payment friction.
  </Step>

  <Step title="Post in the feed daily to keep momentum">
    The feed is where the cohort stays alive between live sessions. Show up consistently with:

    - **Daily prompts** — one question or reflection tied to that week's lesson
    - **Lesson recaps** — after each live session, post a summary of the key takeaways so participants who couldn't attend stay in the loop
    - **Progress posts** — ask participants to share where they're at. Even "I'm behind this week but catching up" is a win.

    The more you post, the more your participants post — and a cohort where the feed is active feels completely different from one where it's quiet.
  </Step>

  <Step title="Use leaderboards to celebrate the most active participants">
    Berrytap's leaderboard tracks engagement points automatically. Check it regularly and call out your top participants by name in the feed. Something like: "These five people have been absolutely showing up this week — let's go 🏆" creates a visible incentive to participate and makes your most engaged members feel seen.

    Gamification does a lot of the retention work for you here. Points and levels give participants a reason to keep engaging even on the weeks when motivation dips.
  </Step>

  <Step title="Close enrollment when the cohort is full or starts">
    Set a cap on enrollment — a cohort with 30 focused participants is better than one with 300 passive ones. When you hit your cap (or when the start date arrives), close enrollment. This also creates scarcity for your next cohort: people who missed this one will be ready to move fast when you announce the next.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## Running the same cohort again

<Tip>
  Re-running a cohort is easy. Your course content, community, and live room setup are all already there. For the next round, simply reopen enrollment, update your community cover image with the new cohort dates, and update the description. You can reuse everything you built — the only thing that changes is the dates and the group of people going through it.
</Tip>

## After the cohort ends

When your cohort wraps up, the community can live on as an alumni space — a place where graduates stay connected, ask questions, and support each other. You can keep it free for alumni or offer a lower-cost ongoing membership. Either way, keeping graduates in a shared space builds a long-term network around your work and makes your next cohort launch much easier to fill.
